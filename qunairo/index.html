Rules

  • ⚖
    Balance. In every row and column, each axis of the Bloch sphere has as many states at one end as at the other: as many |0⟩ as |1⟩, as many |+⟩ as |−⟩, as many |+i⟩ as |−i⟩. The row's average state is then the centre of the sphere.
  • ≢
    No three alike. No three identical states may sit next to each other in a row or column.
  • ○
    ○ identical: the two neighbours are the same state, overlap |⟨a|b⟩|² = 1.
  • ●
    ● opposite: the two ends of one axis, like |0⟩ and |1⟩, overlap 0.
  • ◐
    ◐ unbiased: on different axes, like |0⟩ and |+⟩, overlap ½. Classical bits never have this relation: two bits are either equal or opposite.

The states

  • ↑
    |0⟩ up and |1⟩ down: the Z axis, the only one a classical bit has.
  • →
    |+⟩ right and |−⟩ left: the X axis, on the equator.
  • ⊙
    |+i⟩ toward you (⊙) and |−i⟩ away from you (⊗): the Y axis.

Measurement hints

You can measure a cell along an axis, at most 3 times per puzzle. The puzzle keeps the original state, and you measure a copy. If the state lies on that axis, you always get its own pole. If it doesn't, you get a random 50/50 result. Either way the answer only rules out the opposite pole. That's all one measurement of a qubit can tell you.
